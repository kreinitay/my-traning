How To

The perfect pull-up
  • Grip the bar a bit wider than your shoulders.
  • Start hanging with straight arms.
  • Pull your shoulders down first, then pull until your chin is over the bar.
  • Lower yourself all the way down with control.
  • No kicking or swinging — a clean rep counts, a messy one doesn't.
Pull-up or chin-up?

Chin-up = palms facing you. Pull-up = palms facing away. Chin-ups are easier because your biceps help more. Start with chin-ups if you want and switch later — both count.

Negatives

Jump or step up so your chin is over the bar, then lower yourself as slowly as you can until your arms are straight. Count the seconds out loud.

Top hold

Jump up to the top (chin over bar) and hold it. Squeeze the bar, shoulders down, elbows tight. If you start to slip, lower slowly — that's a bonus negative.

Dead hang & scapular pulls

Dead hang: just hang with straight arms — builds grip. Scapular pull: while hanging, pull your shoulders down away from your ears without bending your arms, then relax.

When to slow down
  • Sharp pain in elbow or shoulder → stop for the day.
  • Still sore on a training day? Take an extra rest day.
  • Couldn't finish a day? Repeat it next time.
  • Can't finish a set? Do the missing reps as negatives.
No bar at home?

A doorway pull-up bar works well — check it's tight before hanging your full weight. A park bar or playground works too.

How to jump higher
  • Swing your arms hard — back on the way down, up on the way up.
  • Quick dip: don't squat too deep before you jump.
  • Every jump at 100% effort. Tired, slow jumps don't make you jump higher — stop the set instead.
  • Strong legs + fast jumps = higher jump. That's why the plan has both.
Land softly (protect your knees)
  • Land on the balls of your feet, then heels.
  • Hips back, knees bend — like sitting into a chair.
  • Knees point the same way as your toes, never caving in.
  • Quiet landings = good landings.
Reach jumps

Pick something high: a door frame, a tree branch, the backboard or net, or tape on a wall. Jump and try to touch it. When it gets easy, pick something higher.

Approach jump (basketball)

Take 2–3 fast steps, make your last two steps quick (right-left or left-right), plant both feet, swing your arms and explode up. This is how you jump highest in a game.

The squats
  • Squat: feet shoulder-width, sit back and down, chest up, stand up strong.
  • Sumo squat: feet wide, toes out.
  • Pause squat: stop 3 seconds at the bottom, then stand up.
  • Bulgarian split squat: back foot on a chair, lower until the back knee almost touches the floor.
  • Cossack squat: feet very wide, squat to one side keeping the other leg straight.
  • Backpack: put books in a backpack to make any squat harder.
Using your dumbbells
  • 4 kg = a 2 kg dumbbell in each hand (or both at your chest for squats).
  • 8 kg = both dumbbells + 4 kg in a backpack.
  • Weeks 1–4 use 4 kg, then 8 kg from week 5. If it's too easy, go slower on the way down.
  • Monday morning is no weights — just your body. Dumbbells are for Saturday, Sunday and Thursday.
  • Dumbbell squat jumps: light weight, max height — never slow, heavy jumps.
Calf raises with your dumbbells
  • 4 kg = both 2 kg dumbbells. 8 kg = both dumbbells + 4 kg in a backpack (books or water bottles).
  • Always on a step edge: heels all the way down for a full stretch, then up as high as you can, hold 1 second.
  • Sunday & Saturday: 8 kg on two legs → one leg → one leg with 4 kg. Monday morning: no weights, one leg on a step.
  • Still too easy? Go slower: 3 seconds down.
The 4 jump tests
  • Vertical jump: hands on hips, no arm swing, jump straight up from standing. Your start: 38.9 cm · goal 45 cm.
  • Highest touch: with arm swing, touch the wall as high as you can. Measure from the floor.
  • Broad jump: two feet → two feet, as far forward as you can. Your start: 172 cm.
  • One-leg broad jump: take off from one leg, land on both. Your start: left 154 cm, right 148 cm. Try to get the weaker leg closer to the stronger one.
  • Do 3 tries and keep the best. Same shoes and same floor every time.
When to slow down
  • Pain under the kneecap or in the ankle → no jumping that day, just do the stretching.
  • Jump on grass, a court or a mat if you can — not concrete.
  • Sleep well. Your legs get stronger while you rest.
The perfect push-up
  • Hands a bit wider than your shoulders, body in one straight line.
  • Chest goes down to about a fist from the floor.
  • Elbows about 45° from your body — not flared out to the sides.
  • Push all the way up. Half reps don't count.
The perfect push-up (goal 10–15)
  • 3 seconds down, slow and controlled.
  • Pause 1 second with your chest just above the floor.
  • Push up strong all the way to straight arms.
  • Body straight like a plank the whole time — no sagging hips, no bum in the air.
  • Elbows about 45° from your body. If one rep isn't perfect, the set is over.
How the plan works
  • Goals: 40–50 push-ups in one go, and 10–15 perfect push-ups.
  • Every number comes from your max and your perfect max. Until you test: max 25, perfect 8.
  • You test both (perfect first, then max) on Nov 9, Dec 7, Jan 4, Feb 1 and the final tests on Feb 24. After each test all the sets get bigger.
  • Max Day (Mon): sets that go down (80% → 50% of your max), then one max set, then slow push-ups.
  • Variety (Tue): lighter — different kinds of push-ups, because you also trained on Monday.
  • Pump Day (Wed): sets until your arms are full (about 15–20) and pyramids.
  • Bonus Day: every 2 weeks, on your Jump High day off (Sunday or Friday) — ladders and every-minute sets.
The variations
  • Wide: hands much wider than shoulders — more chest.
  • Diamond: hands together under your chest, thumbs and fingers make a diamond — more triceps.
  • Decline: feet on a chair or bed — harder, more shoulders.
  • Archer: very wide hands, go down to one side with the other arm straight.
  • Pike: hips high like an upside-down V, lower your head toward the floor — shoulders.
  • Clap / explosive: push up so hard your hands leave the floor. Land soft.
  • Pseudo-planche: hands by your belly, fingers pointing to your feet, lean forward.
  • Slow: 3–4 seconds down, normal speed up.
"Until your arms are full"

Keep going until your arms feel full and heavy — about 15–20 for you now. Stop 1–2 reps before you can't do any more. That burning feeling is the "pump".

3 days in a row

Push-ups are Mon, Tue and Wed. Tuesday is lighter on purpose so your arms can recover. If your wrists or shoulders hurt, skip Tuesday and do Wednesday.
